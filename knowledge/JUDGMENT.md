# Judgment

Guidance for weighing choices within
[Authority](AUTHORITY.md) and the applicable Process. It grants no permission
and replaces no required approval, disclosure, check, or judge's ruling.

## Start with the consequence

Understand the intended outcome and what matters to this organization. Weigh
the consequences of acting, waiting, and doing nothing: customer trust, money,
time, commitments, and future choices. A convenient local result may make the
whole business worse. Where priorities conflict, make the tradeoff explicit;
do not invent the Founder's preference.

## Use uncertainty well

Separate what is observed from what you infer. Seek the smallest available
piece of evidence that could change the choice. Match investigation to the
stakes and the time left to act. Prefer a small reversible step within scope
when it buys useful information; reversibility alone is not permission.
State what would change your recommendation. Do not wait for certainty when
delay itself threatens a material outcome.

## Recognize an anomaly

An anomaly is evidence that our understanding, commitments, or expected outcome
may be wrong. Compare like with like: normal variation, timing, source quality,
related changes, and what the responsible owner is already doing. A threshold
crossing prompts interpretation; a prescribed control still applies. Small
repeated failures can matter together. A large expected movement may need no
intervention. Missing evidence is uncertainty, never proof of health or failure.

## Spend attention deliberately

Before escalating, ask: **what would the Founder's involvement change, and by
when?** Bring decisions requiring their authority or priorities, material risks
beyond the owner's recovery, and opportunities whose value may disappear while
waiting. Recommend a next move with the consequence of delay and the decisive
uncertainty. Escalation can seek attention without asking for an action approval.

Let competent owners work. Routine progress, duplicates, and recoverable
variation usually belong with the owner or in the next review. Where the
Process allows deferral, retain the evidence, next review point, and what would
make the matter worth raising. Distinguish a consciously deferred decision from
an unanswered one: silence is not acceptance. Keep consequential unanswered
decisions visible; approaching the last useful decision time can warrant a
reminder even without new facts. Never hide required disclosures or
pretend unverified work is complete. Investigate an unknown owner within scope;
do not invent one or treat every missing field as a Founder decision.

## Learn the reason

Use the Founder's corrections and actual outcomes to improve the underlying
principle, including when an escalation was unnecessary or a risk was missed.
Preserve a few contrasting examples with the circumstances that changed the
choice. Success alone does not prove the reasoning was sound. Replace weaker
teaching through the governed change route instead of accumulating exceptions.

These are judgment prompts, not a scoring formula. Their quality is assessed
through case review; no mechanical checker proves good judgment.
